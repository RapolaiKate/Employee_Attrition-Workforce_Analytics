# Power BI Dashboard – Employee Attrition & Workforce Analytics

## 📊 Overview

This folder contains the **Power BI dashboard** developed for the Employee Attrition and Workforce Analytics project.

The dashboard transforms the cleaned employee dataset into an interactive business intelligence report that allows users to explore **employee attrition, workforce characteristics, job satisfaction, overtime, income, tenure, and other HR metrics**.

The objective is to provide HR stakeholders with a clear and interactive view of workforce trends that can support **data-driven decision-making and employee retention strategies**.

---

## 🎯 Dashboard Objectives

The Power BI dashboard was designed to:

* Monitor overall employee attrition.
* Track key workforce KPIs.
* Identify departments with higher attrition.
* Compare attrition across job levels.
* Analyze the relationship between overtime and attrition.
* Explore job satisfaction and work-life balance.
* Analyze income and employee tenure.
* Examine promotion and performance patterns.
* Provide interactive filtering for deeper analysis.

# 📊 Dashboard Analysis

The Power BI report provides visual analysis of several workforce dimensions.

### 👥 Employee Attrition

The dashboard provides an overview of employees who stayed versus employees who left the organization.

### 🏢 Department Analysis

Departments can be compared based on employee numbers and attrition rates.

Engineering recorded the highest department attrition at **9.58%**, while HR recorded the lowest at **7.27%**.

### 📈 Job Level Analysis

Attrition is compared across employee job levels.

Level 1 recorded the highest attrition at **11.22%**, while Level 4 recorded the lowest at **5.04%**.

### ⏰ Overtime Analysis

The dashboard compares attrition between employees who work overtime and those who do not.

* Overtime: **12.18%**
* No overtime: **7.78%**

This represents a **4.40 percentage-point difference**.

### 😊 Job Satisfaction

Employee satisfaction levels are analyzed against attrition.

* Satisfaction Level 2: **16.63%**
* Satisfaction Level 5: **7.17%**

### ⚖️ Work-Life Balance

The dashboard compares attrition across work-life-balance levels.

* Level 2: **12.18%**
* Level 4: **6.86%**

### 💰 Monthly Income

Income groups are analyzed to identify differences in employee attrition.

* 3,000–5,999: **11.15%**
* 6,000–8,999: **10.00%**
* 9,000+: **7.28%**

### 🕒 Years at Company

Employee tenure is compared against attrition.

* 0–2 years: **10.60%**
* 11+ years: **5.90%**

### 🎓 Promotion

Employees with and without recent promotions are compared.

* No recent promotion: **9.27%**
* Recent promotion: **6.67%**

# 🔥 Key Business Insights

The Power BI dashboard highlights several important patterns:

1. **Overall attrition was 9.03%**, with 903 employees leaving out of 10,000.
2. **Engineering had the highest department attrition** at 9.58%.
3. **Level 1 employees had the highest job-level attrition** at 11.22%.
4. Employees working **overtime had higher attrition** than employees who did not work overtime.
5. **Lower job satisfaction was associated with higher attrition.**
6. Lower work-life-balance scores were associated with higher attrition.
7. Employees in lower income groups showed higher attrition than the highest income group.
8. **Shorter-tenure employees showed higher attrition** than long-tenured employees.
9. Employees with recent promotions showed lower attrition than employees without recent promotions.

# 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the patterns identified in the dashboard, HR teams could consider:

### 1. Monitor overtime

Employees working overtime showed higher attrition. Workload and working-hour patterns should be reviewed.

### 2. Focus on employee satisfaction

Lower satisfaction levels were associated with higher attrition, making employee engagement an important area for investigation.

### 3. Strengthen early-career retention

Level 1 and shorter-tenure employees showed higher attrition. Mentorship, onboarding, and career development could be explored.

### 4. Support career progression

Employees with recent promotions showed lower attrition, suggesting that career development opportunities may be worth investigating.

### 5. Review compensation

Higher income groups showed lower attrition, making compensation an important factor to consider alongside other workforce characteristics.

---

# ⚠️ Analytical Note

The dashboard presents **associations observed in the dataset**, not proof of direct causation.

For example, the higher attrition rate among employees working overtime does not necessarily mean overtime directly causes employees to leave. Other employee and organizational factors may contribute.
